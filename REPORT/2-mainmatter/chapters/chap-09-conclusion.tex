\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:conc:summary}

This study has developed, validated, and deployed a digital-twin simulation
framework for Multi-Echelon Inventory Optimisation (MEIO) with
transport-aware joint optimisation.  The main contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A modular, open digital-twin framework} implementing
        seven inventory control policies (base-stock, $(s,S)$, $(R,Q)$,
        $(R,S)$, order-up-to with phase offset, $(k,m)$-cycle, and
        echelon base-stock), an arbitrary DAG-topology network model,
        and a transport layer with simultaneous volume and weight
        capacity constraints.  The framework is validated by
        84 pytest tests covering simulator invariants,
        policy behaviour, transport mechanics, and the corrected
        bullwhip metric.

  \item \textbf{An Optuna-TPE joint optimiser} that simultaneously
        searches over 25 integer base-stock levels and 5 continuous
        dispatch thresholds in a 30-dimensional mixed-integer space,
        subject to either an aggregate or a per-SKU minimum fill-rate
        constraint.

  \item \textbf{A two-objective NSGA-II Pareto study and a per-SKU
        fill-rate constraint mode}, which together replaced the
        original single-objective constraint sweep with a
        methodologically clean trade-off frontier (smooth and monotone
        in cost vs.\ achieved fill rate, verified by an automated
        decision protocol).

  \item \textbf{Empirical demonstration of the 37\% cost-reduction
        achievable through joint inventory--transport optimisation.}
        On a 5-SKU, 1N3 ($N=2$) network with M5 Walmart demand, the
        joint optimiser (E3) achieves:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item Total cost \$261,248 vs \$416,952 at baseline
                ($-$37.3\%, saving \$155,704).
          \item Fill rate 98.72\% vs 91.59\% at baseline ($+$7.13\,pp),
                comfortably above the 92\% service-level target.
          \item Transport cost reduced from 79.5\% to 52.7\% of total,
                driven by a 3.5:1 leverage ratio between holding and
                transport-plus-shortage savings.
        \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{A Pareto frontier mapping the cost--service trade-off}
        across non-dominated points from 93.9\% to 99.8\% fill,
        revealing a knee around 97--98\% with marginal cost rising from
        \$8k/percentage-point below 97\% to \$15--20k/percentage-point
        between 98\% and 99.8\%.

  \item \textbf{Four extension experiments (E5--E8)} covering
        disruption robustness, policy comparison, forecast
        sensitivity, and bullwhip-aware optimisation, demonstrating:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item E5: the joint optimum's incidental safety-stock buffer
                absorbs a 14-day W1 outage 8\,pp better in fill rate
                and 13.8\% cheaper in cost than the analytical
                baseline, despite the optimiser never seeing the
                disruption.
          \item E6 (re-run with joint transport co-optimisation per
                policy): base-stock is most cost-effective (\$288k /
                99.29\%), followed by $(s,S)$ (\$302k) and
                echelon-stock (\$307k); periodic-review fails the 92\%
                fill target even with joint dispatch optimisation.
                Echelon-stock's large disadvantage in prior
                inventory-only comparisons nearly vanishes when
                all policies have equal access to transport
                consolidation.  Joint base-stock + dispatch (E3,
                \$261k) remains the best overall configuration.
          \item E7: joint optima are robust to forecast error up to
                $\sigma_f \approx 10\%$ ($\sim 4\%$ cost increase) and
                degrade gracefully through 20--30\%.
          \item E8: under base-stock with deterministic lead times,
                the per-echelon bullwhip metric is $\approx 1$ in
                steady state; bullwhip in this framework is induced
                by policy choice (E6) and supply shocks (E5), not by
                demand.
        \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Stochastic demand robustness (E9).}  The E3 jointly-optimised
        parameters were evaluated across ten independently seeded Poisson
        demand realisations ($\lambda$ = M5 mean per retailer/SKU).
        Mean fill rate was 99.95\%\,$\pm$\,0.03\% and mean total cost
        was \$242k\,$\pm$\,\$1k — confirming that the deterministically-optimised
        parameters remain effective under stochastic demand.

  \item \textbf{A CSV-driven configuration pipeline} lowering the
        barrier for practitioners to define and experiment with new
        scenarios without editing JSON directly.

  \item \textbf{Preliminary validation experiments (EV1--EV4)}
        confirming simulator correctness against known theoretical
        behaviours: order convergence under constant demand, demand
        shock propagation, policy comparison under transport
        capacity, and non-linear capacity scaling.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc:futurework}

The framework is designed to be extensible.  Several directions are
proposed for future development.

\begin{description}
  \item[Disruption-aware joint optimisation.] E5 characterised the
        \emph{incidental} robustness of the cost-only optimum.  An
        optimiser that explicitly evaluates each candidate against a
        disruption scenario during search would in principle find a
        more robust configuration, possibly at a small steady-state
        cost premium.

  \item[Stochastic lead times.] The current simulator uses
        deterministic transit times.  Extending to stochastic lead
        times (e.g.\ Gamma- or Uniform-distributed) would allow the
        framework to model carrier variability and may also be the
        regime in which echelon base-stock regains the empirical
        advantage that E6 denied it under deterministic lead times.

  \item[Live demand forecasting.] E7 demonstrates robustness to
        forecast error in the abstract.  Replacing the noisy-oracle
        wrapper with a live online forecaster (exponential smoothing,
        LightGBM, or neural-network point and interval forecasts)
        would close the loop and allow a complete forecast-and-fulfil
        pipeline to be optimised.

  \item[Lateral transshipment between retailers.] Retailers cannot
        currently transfer stock to each other.  Lateral transshipment
        is a common strategy for balancing inventory across retail
        locations and could substantially reduce shortage costs,
        particularly under disruption.  Adding a transshipment option
        to the edge model and a transshipment-aware policy would
        extend the framework to cover this common industry practice.

  \item[Larger and richer network topologies.] The canonical 1N3
        topology has $N=2$ warehouses serving the three retailers in
        a fixed assignment.  Multi-tier hub-and-spoke networks,
        multiple suppliers, or lateral assignment (a retailer sourced
        by either warehouse) may exhibit different consolidation
        dynamics that this study has not characterised.

  \item[Stress regimes for bullwhip.] E8 showed that bullwhip is
        uninformative under base-stock + deterministic lead times —
        the per-echelon ratio is $\approx 1$ in steady state, leaving
        the bullwhip-aware optimiser nothing to act on in this
        scenario.  Reporting bullwhip in the canonical scenario
        therefore measures absence of amplification rather than its
        degree.  Stress regimes in which bullwhip is non-trivial —
        e.g.\ promotional demand spikes, smoothed forecasts, periodic
        review or $(s,S)$ as the dominant policy, or stochastic lead
        times — would give the metric and the bullwhip-aware
        optimiser meaningful work to do.

  \item[Stochastic demand optimisation.] E9 demonstrated that the E3
        parameters \emph{transfer} to Poisson demand without
        re-optimisation.  The natural extension is to optimise
        directly under stochastic demand — replacing the deterministic
        M5 trace with a Poisson or bootstrapped generator — to
        determine whether the Poisson-optimal parameters differ
        meaningfully from the deterministic ones and to produce
        credible intervals on cost and fill-rate estimates.

  \item[Scalability beyond 30 dimensions.] The joint search space for
        the canonical 1N3, 5-SKU scenario is 30-dimensional; TPE is
        tractable at this scale within 100--200 trials.  A 10-node,
        10-SKU network would produce a $\sim$105-dimensional joint
        space, beyond the $\sim$50-dimension practical limit of the
        current implementation.  Hierarchical decomposition
        (optimise echelons independently then coordinate at
        boundaries), warm-starting from analytical bounds, or
        per-SKU-family decomposition would be required to scale the
        approach to larger networks.

  \item[Reinforcement-learning policies.] RL policies (e.g.\ Proximal
        Policy Optimisation) could be trained within the simulator to
        discover adaptive ordering strategies that the analytical
        policy group cannot represent.

  \item[Web dashboard.] A lightweight web interface allowing
        practitioners to configure scenarios, trigger optimisation
        runs, and explore results without command-line interaction
        would broaden the framework's accessibility.

  \item[Industry-specific configuration templates.] Pre-built JSON
        and CSV templates for common supply-chain archetypes (grocery
        retail, pharmaceutical distribution, e-commerce fulfilment)
        would reduce setup time for new users.
\end{description}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc:closing}

The central finding of this study is simple to state but non-obvious in
practice: \emph{inventory and transport are not independent levers.}
Holding extra safety stock is conventionally viewed as a cost to be
minimised; this work shows that, in a transport-cost-dominated network,
extra holding cost is a strategic investment that enables vehicle
consolidation and generates a ${\sim}3.5{:}1$ return in
transport-plus-shortage savings.

The extension experiments add nuance.  The joint optimum's safety-stock
buffer incidentally absorbs supply disruptions (E5).  When all four
inventory policies are given equal access to transport consolidation
(E6, re-run jointly), base-stock remains the most cost-effective
structure; echelon-stock's classical disadvantage nearly disappears.
The E9 stochastic robustness test confirms that the
deterministically-optimised parameters remain effective under Poisson
demand.  The recommendations are robust to realistic
forecast error (E7).  And the per-echelon bullwhip metric, when
computed correctly, reveals that this scenario simply does not amplify
demand variability — bullwhip is policy-induced and shock-induced, not
demand-induced.

The digital-twin framework built in this project provides a portable,
testable, and configurable tool for supply-chain design.  By exposing
the cost--service Pareto frontier, characterising disruption robustness,
comparing policies on equal footing, and reporting per-echelon bullwhip,
it moves supply-chain decisions from intuition to evidence.
