\begin{onehalfspacing}
Classical multi-echelon inventory models optimise stock levels in isolation,
treating transport as a linear per-unit cost.  In practice, vehicle-based
transport has a step-function cost structure: a half-full truck costs nearly
the same to operate as a full one, so per-unit transport cost is dictated by
vehicle utilisation rather than quantity shipped.  When transport accounts
for the majority of total supply-chain cost, this mismatch renders
inventory-only or transport-only optimisation fundamentally suboptimal.

This project develops a modular, open-source \textbf{digital-twin simulation
framework} for Multi-Echelon Inventory Optimisation (MEIO) that addresses
this gap by co-optimising inventory and transport decisions jointly.  The
framework implements a discrete-event simulation engine with a daily-tick
loop, seven inventory control policies (base-stock, $(s,S)$, $(R,Q)$,
$(R,S)$, order-up-to with phase offset, $(k,m)$-cycle, and echelon
base-stock), an arbitrary directed-acyclic-graph network topology, and a
transport layer with simultaneous volume and weight capacity constraints.  A
Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) optimiser, implemented via Optuna,
searches a 20-dimensional mixed-integer space of base-stock levels and
dispatch thresholds, subject to a configurable minimum fill-rate constraint.

Experiments are conducted on a 5-SKU, one-supplier–one-warehouse–three-retailer
(1N3) network using real demand data from the M5 Walmart Forecasting dataset.
The headline result is that joint inventory-transport optimisation (E3) achieves
a total cost of \$1{,}556{,}516 --- a \textbf{39\% reduction} relative to
the analytical Clark-Scarf baseline (\$2{,}553{,}418), while simultaneously
raising the fill rate from 86.15\% to 95.37\%, comfortably above the 92\%
service-level target.  The mechanism is a deliberate increase in warehouse
safety stock (+\$242k holding cost) that enables high vehicle-consolidation
thresholds, reducing transport cost by \$1.04M and yielding a \textbf{4.3:1
leverage ratio} between holding and transport savings.

A Pareto frontier analysis across eight fill-rate targets (80\%--98\%) reveals
a natural operating point at approximately 95\% fill / \$1.56M and sharply
diminishing returns above 94\%, providing a principled basis for negotiating
service-level agreements.  Statistical robustness is confirmed via multi-seed
validation with 95\% confidence intervals and Welch's $t$-tests.

The framework is validated by approximately 80 pytest unit and integration
tests and is fully reproducible from a self-contained configuration and
demand-data pipeline.
\end{onehalfspacing}
